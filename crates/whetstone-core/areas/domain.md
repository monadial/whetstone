Focus on the subject the change is about: the concepts it names, the rules
it encodes and the language it uses for them.

Look for:

- The concepts the change introduces, renames or redefines, and how they
  relate to the ones already there.
- The rules and invariants it adds or changes: what must always hold, what
  is forbidden, and which states or cases are possible.
- Edge cases in the subject itself: unusual but legitimate situations the
  rules must cover, and what happens at their boundaries.
- Whether each word matches what it stands for, and means the same thing
  everywhere it appears.
- What the change assumes about the people, organisations or things it
  describes, and whether those assumptions hold.

Prefer why the rules are the way they are, and what would go wrong if they
were different, over naming style, formatting or tooling.

When the repository is not software, such as a course, a book or a set of
policies, its domain is its subject: the ideas, definitions, obligations and
cases it sets out, and whether the change states them accurately and
consistently.
